Most GPU deployments are sized wrong in one of two directions. Size from the spec sheet and you pay for power you never draw. Size from a quiet benchmark and your breakers trip on the first real training run. This guide walks through a method that lands in between, in five steps you can do with a spreadsheet.

1. Start from measured draw, not the nameplate

The number printed on a power supply is the most it can deliver, not what your server uses. A server with four 3 kW supplies does not draw 12 kW. Some of those supplies are there for redundancy, and the rest rarely run flat out.

Measure instead. Run your heaviest real workload, usually a full training job with every GPU busy, and read peak draw from the server's management controller or a metered PDU. No hardware yet? Use the vendor's maximum-configuration power figure, which is closer to reality than the nameplate.

2. Add everything else in the rack

GPUs get the attention, but they are not alone in the cabinet. Add:

  • Top-of-rack and management switches.
  • Storage nodes and any head or login servers.
  • Firewalls, console servers and out-of-band gear.

Each is small on its own. Together they often add a kilowatt or more.

3. Leave headroom

Electrical circuits should not run at 100% of their rating for hours at a time. A common rule is to keep continuous load at or below 80% of the breaker rating. On top of that, leave room for growth: the next GPU generation tends to draw more, not less.

4. Match power to cooling

Every kilowatt of power you draw becomes a kilowatt of heat. A rack plan that ignores cooling is not a plan. This is why density is a property of the building, not just the rack: TOR2 at 151 Front is cooled by Enwave's deep lake water system and supports up to 25 kW per rack.

5. Plan the feeds

High-density cabinets in TOR2 run on 208 V three-phase power. Spread your servers so the load is balanced across the three phases, and plug redundant supplies into separate A and B feeds so a single feed can fail without taking a server down.

A worked example

Say you plan three 8-GPU servers. The figures below are illustrative; use your own measurements.

  • Three servers at 6.5 kW measured peak each: 19.5 kW.
  • Two switches and a storage node: 1.5 kW.
  • Total: 21 kW, which fits one 25 kW cabinet and leaves 4 kW for growth.

Add a fourth server and the same math says two cabinets. That is a conversation worth having before the hardware ships, not after.
