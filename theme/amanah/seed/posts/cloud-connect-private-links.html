Most teams do not choose between colocation and cloud any more. They run both: databases and steady workloads in a rack they control, bursty and managed services in the cloud. The weak point is usually the link between the two. This month we opened Cloud Connect to every colocation customer at 151 Front Street West, so that link can be private.

What Cloud Connect is

Cloud Connect is a private connection from your rack to a cloud provider. Traffic between your servers and your cloud account does not cross the public internet. It travels over a dedicated path from 151 Front to the provider's network.

  • Four clouds: AWS, Azure, Google Cloud and Cloudflare.
  • Speeds from 100 Mbps to 10 Gbps, sized to your traffic.
  • From $500 CAD / month, on the same invoice as your rack.

Why private beats public

Predictable latency

Public internet routes change without notice. A private link takes the same path every time, so the time between your database and your application stays steady. That matters most for chatty workloads, like an app tier in the cloud talking to a database in your rack.

Lower data transfer bills

Cloud providers charge to move data out of their networks. Moving the heavy, steady part of a workload into a rack, and keeping a private line to what stays in the cloud, changes that math. Qublix Games reports a 10 to 15% lower AWS bill since moving to Amanah.

A smaller attack surface

Traffic that never touches the public internet is traffic nobody can scan from it. Pair a private link with a rack behind biometric access and 24/7 on-site staff, and both ends of the connection are in places you can account for.

How teams use it

  • Hybrid apps: the database in your rack, the web tier and managed services in the cloud.
  • Backups and archives: nightly copies to cloud storage without saturating your internet transit.
  • Burst compute: steady work runs in the rack; peaks spill into the cloud over a link you already trust.
  • Edge delivery: origin servers at 151 Front, with Cloudflare in front of them.
