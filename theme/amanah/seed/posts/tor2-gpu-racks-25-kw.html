Why density matters

A typical colocation rack is sized for 5 to 8 kW. A single 8-GPU server can draw more than 10 kW on its own under training load. In a low-density hall, that forces you to spread one cluster across half-empty racks. You pay for floor space you cannot use, you run more cables between nodes, and you add latency where your workload is most sensitive to it.

At 25 kW a cabinet, the same cluster sits in fewer racks, closer together, on shorter runs. That is the whole point of TOR2.

Cooled by Lake Ontario

Power is only half of density. Every kilowatt you put into a rack comes back out as heat, and heat is what usually caps a hall. 151 Front is cooled through Enwave's deep lake water system, which draws cold water from Lake Ontario. That is what lets TOR2 hold dense cabinets at full load, all year.
