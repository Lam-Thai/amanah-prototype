What a SOC 2 Type II report means for your auditors

A plain-language guide to what our SOC 2 Type II report covers, what stays your responsibility, and how to use it in your next audit.

Sooner or later, an auditor asks where your servers live and how you know that place is secure. “It is a good data centre” is not an answer they can file. A SOC 2 Type II report is. This guide explains what ours covers, what it does not, and how to hand it to your auditors so the question closes quickly.

SOC 2 in one paragraph

SOC 2 is an audit framework from the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants. An independent auditor checks a service provider’s controls against a set of trust services criteria: security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality and privacy. The provider chooses which criteria are in scope. Our report covers security and availability.

Type I versus Type II

  • Type I says the controls were designed properly on one day.
  • Type II says the controls actually worked, tested over a period of months.

The difference matters. Anyone can write a good access policy. A Type II report shows the auditor sampled real access logs, real visitor records and real incident tickets across a twelve-month period, and found the policy was followed.

What our report covers at 151 Front

The report covers the building and the services we run in it, including:

  • Physical access: biometric readers, logged entry and staff on site around the clock.
  • Power: N+1 UPS with generator failover, and how it is maintained and tested.
  • Environment: cooling through Enwave’s deep lake water system and how it is monitored.
  • Operations: how remote hands requests, cross connects and incidents are handled and recorded.

What stays your responsibility

Our report covers our controls, not yours. Your operating systems, applications, user accounts, encryption and backups are outside it. The report lists complementary user entity controls: things you must do for the overall picture to hold, like keeping your list of authorised visitors current. Your auditor will look for evidence that you do them.

Canadian privacy, too

If you handle personal information, your auditors will also ask about Canadian law. Your data stays in Toronto, at 151 Front Street West, and our controls align with PIPEDA and with PHIPA for personal health information in Ontario.

How to use it in your audit

  1. Request the report. We share it with customers and serious prospects under a non-disclosure agreement.
  2. Check the period. Make sure it overlaps your audit period. If there is a gap, ask us for a bridge letter.
  3. Map the user controls. Match each complementary user entity control to something your team already does.
  4. Book a walk-through. Auditors who want to see the floor are welcome on a tour.
